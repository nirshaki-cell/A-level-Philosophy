Kantian deontological ethics

Morality is not about producing good outcomes but about acting from duty, on principles any rational being could will as law. This tour follows Kant’s Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785): the good will, acting from duty, hypothetical and categorical imperatives, and the first two formulations of the categorical imperative. It then covers the objections on the specification and some that go beyond it.

Built from the departmental slides Kantian Ethics: Duty, reason and the good will and Johnson and Cureton, ‘Kant’s Moral Philosophy’, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Winter 2025). Quotations from the Groundwork are given with Akademie page numbers (e.g. 4:394). Material marked ‘Added’ was not in those sources and should be checked.
