Religious language

When we say ‘God loves us’, are we stating a fact – or doing something else entirely? Take the tour: cognitivism and non-cognitivism, Ayer’s verification principle, Hick’s reply, and the ‘University debate’ between Flew, Mitchell and Hare – with Wittgenstein and Aquinas beyond the spec.

Built from the departmental resources: Religious Language and Mitchell and Hare slides (2024), the religious language revision table, Wittgenstein on religious language and belief, and Scott, ‘Religious Language’, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (2022).
