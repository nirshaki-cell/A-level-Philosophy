Aristotelian virtue ethics

Aristotle doesn’t start by asking “what should I do?” He asks “how should I live, and what kind of person should I be?” This tour follows the Nicomachean Ethics: eudaimonia, the function argument, virtue as a disposition, habituation and the mean, voluntary action and practical wisdom. It then covers the four issues on the specification and goes beyond them.

Built from the departmental slides Aristotle’s ethics I–II, Aristotle on action and Nicomachean Ethics Books 6, 7 and 10, and Kraut, ‘Aristotle’s Ethics’, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Quotations from the Nicomachean Ethics are from W. D. Ross’s translation, with Bekker references (e.g. 1094a1). Material marked ‘Added’ was not in those sources and should be checked. Material marked ‘Beyond the spec’ is extension only. AQA 7172 · Moral Philosophy.
